# feat: upgrade paired relay connections to a direct WebRTC link

Status: approved

## Provenance

- main: `9b6df0113` — 2026-10-03
- upstream-rebrand: `bdc3888cf` — 2026-09-30
- upstream/main: `c481ecf3e` (v0.10.0) — 2026-09-28

## Scope

**In scope:**

1. One chunker on the plaintext side of the existing E2EE channel, on both ends of a relay connection, serving both the relay and the DataChannel: it splits every app message into chunks, each encrypted on its own, in send order, and carries the direct-link signaling and the cutover marker as encrypted messages of its own on the relay leg.
2. A direct carrier under the E2EE channel, on both ends, that moves ciphertext frames from the relay to the DataChannel at cutover.
3. Negotiating the chunker and carrier in the existing E2EE `e2ee_hello` / `e2ee_ready` capabilities, so old peers never see either.
4. A WebRTC DataChannel peer on the daemon (node-datachannel, in both the CLI daemon and the desktop app's bundled daemon), on iOS and Android 13 and newer (our Expo module over `tel.schich:libdatachannel-java-android` from Maven Central and the `libdatachannel` CocoaPod), and on web and Electron (built-in `RTCPeerConnection`).
5. Cutover from relay to the DataChannel, fallback to relay when ICE fails, and a full reconnect when the DataChannel drops.
6. STUN at the relay host, port 3478, derived from the relay endpoint on each end. No other STUN server.
7. A STUN-only server as its own container on the relay host, added to the getrambla repo's `relay-setup` and `relay-update` recipes (not public), with UDP 3478 open for IPv4 and IPv6 in the relay host's firewall, rate-limited per source, and checked by its `relay-test` recipe.
8. Settings, Connections labels a connection on the DataChannel `WebRTC` in place of `Relay`, and so does the host page's active connection badge.
9. Turning the relay off on the daemon also disconnects clients on the DataChannel.
10. A public docs subsection on the direct link, STUN, and self-hosting it.
11. The F-Droid profile's Expo autolinking finds the app's own modules in `packages/app/modules/`, including `rambla-datachannel`.

**Not in scope:**

- Any change to the rambla-relay repo.
- TURN.
- Git submodules, or building libdatachannel from source.
- New protocol messages, `server_info.features` flags, or changes to `messages.ts`, `session.ts`, or `websocket-server.ts`; `daemon-client.ts` changes only by the chunker hook and 1 public getter for the client's transport factory.
- Changing direct (non-relay) connections.
- Retrying a failed ICE attempt within the same connection, ICE restart, or live fallback to relay without a reconnect.
- Streaming partial messages to the app (a large message still arrives whole).
- Color, formatting, or translation of the `WebRTC` label, and pairing text about the upgrade.
- A working F-Droid build: an F-Droid-profile build that succeeds end to end, its F-Droid recipe, and publishing on F-Droid. A later plan covers it.
- 16 KB page alignment. `tel.schich:libdatachannel-java-android` ships 4 KB-aligned `.so` files, short of Google Play's 16 KB requirement ([page sizes](https://developer.android.com/guide/practices/page-sizes)). This is a known limitation, reported to its author, and out of scope for this plan and its review.

## Acceptance criteria

1. An iOS, Android 13 and newer, web, or Electron client paired over relay, on networks where hole punching works, moves app traffic to the DataChannel within 10 s of connecting; after cutover the relay data socket carries no app frames.
2. When ICE fails, the connection stays on relay and works as today, with no error shown. On iOS, the one-time local network permission prompt is allowed; if the user denies it, the connection stays on relay.
3. Old client with new daemon, and new client with old daemon, connect and work over relay as today.
4. A message larger than the DataChannel max size (a 5 MiB checkout diff) arrives intact over the DataChannel.
5. An automated test streams numbered messages through the cutover and receives all of them, in order.
6. Killing the DataChannel after cutover triggers the existing reconnect; the client reconnects on relay and upgrades again.
7. The DataChannel carries only ciphertext frames from the existing E2EE channel.
8. Android gets libdatachannel from Maven Central (`tel.schich:libdatachannel-java-android`) and iOS from CocoaPods (`libdatachannel`). The Android release build without the F-Droid profile succeeds. Under the F-Droid profile, Expo autolinking lists `rambla-datachannel` and the app's other local Android modules; the F-Droid build itself need not succeed.
9. `npm run typecheck`, `npm run lint`, and the new `*.rambla.test.ts` files pass.
10. A phone connected through the relay to the desktop app's bundled daemon moves to the DataChannel, checked by hand on the Linux x64 build. On macOS arm64, macOS x64, Linux x64, and Windows x64, the packaged app loads node-datachannel and creates a peer connection that produces a local offer.
11. Each end uses STUN at the relay host it connects to, port 3478, served by its own STUN-only container on that host, with the firewall rate-limiting each source. No other STUN server. No TURN.
12. One chunker serves both the relay and the DataChannel; no chunk is larger than 64 KiB.
13. The phone module's native tests run in `mobile-native-tests.rambla.yml` on every push and pass.
14. Web and Electron clients use the built-in `RTCPeerConnection`; node-datachannel runs only in the CLI daemon and the desktop app's bundled daemon.
15. In Settings, Connections, on iOS, Android, web, and Electron, a relay connection that has moved to the DataChannel shows `WebRTC` in place of `Relay`, and shows `Relay` again after it falls back to relay. The active connection badge at the top of the host page does the same. The label and badge change the moment the connection switches.
16. Turning the relay off on the daemon disconnects every client connected through it, including clients on the DataChannel, on any platform.
17. Only the connection the app uses as live sets the Settings label and badge. A speed-check connection that moves to the DataChannel leaves them unchanged; once the app adopts it as live, they show `WebRTC`.
18. `public-docs/connectivity.md` explains the direct link and relay fallback, STUN on UDP 3478 at the relay host, and how a self-hoster sets up STUN and the relay endpoints.
19. `just relay-test` in the getrambla repo checks the relay host's STUN server on UDP 3478. A binding request over IPv4 and one over IPv6 each get an answer, and a TURN allocation request in the same run gets no answer. Any failure fails the recipe.
20. An Android 10-12 phone offers no direct-link capability, never loads libdatachannel, and works on relay as today, with no error shown.

## Goal

Paired clients talk to the daemon over a direct WebRTC DataChannel when hole punching works, cutting latency and relay traffic, and stay on relay when it does not. One chunker serves both carriers.

## Constraints

- `packages/relay/src/encrypted-channel.ts` does not change, and the relay does not change. The carrier adds and reads its capability on the `e2ee_hello` / `e2ee_ready` frames ([../packages/relay/src/encrypted-channel.ts:75](../packages/relay/src/encrypted-channel.ts#L75)).
- Without the negotiated capability, the carrier and the chunker pass every frame other than the `e2ee_hello` / `e2ee_ready` handshake frames through byte for byte, in order, and a send still waits for the relay socket's completion callback ([../packages/server/src/server/relay-transport.test.ts:258](../packages/server/src/server/relay-transport.test.ts#L258) asserts that wait).
- Every relay client config the app builds gets the wrapper: the relay branch of `createClient` ([../packages/app/src/runtime/host-runtime.ts:564](../packages/app/src/runtime/host-runtime.ts#L564)) and `buildClientConfig` in `test-daemon-connection.ts`, whose clients the app adopts as live ([../packages/app/src/runtime/host-runtime.ts:1272](../packages/app/src/runtime/host-runtime.ts#L1272)). The wrapper carries the WebSocket factory each site uses today: `createAppWebSocketFactory()` in `createClient` ([../packages/app/src/runtime/host-runtime.ts:517](../packages/app/src/runtime/host-runtime.ts#L517)), and the client default in `buildClientConfig` ([../packages/client/src/daemon-client.ts:1356](../packages/client/src/daemon-client.ts#L1356)). The relay leg opens exactly as today on every platform.
- Only transports made by the transport factory of the client the app adopts as live ([../packages/app/src/runtime/host-runtime.ts:1271](../packages/app/src/runtime/host-runtime.ts#L1271)) set a store entry, and only while on the DataChannel. `switchToConnection` gets that factory through the new `DaemonClient` getter when it adopts the client as live, and marks it; a marked factory sets the entry for a transport already on the DataChannel and for every transport it makes later, including after a reconnect. A marked transport clears its entry on fallback and on any close; no unmarked transport sets or clears it.
- The daemon hook is the `attachEncryptedSocket` call ([../packages/server/src/server/relay-transport.ts:383](../packages/server/src/server/relay-transport.ts#L383)): the carrier wraps the socket that feeds the E2EE channel (line 424), and the chunker wraps the plaintext socket handed to `attachSocket` (line 449). The buffered amount read at line 446 includes the carrier's queue, and the 64 MiB high-water close ([../packages/server/src/server/websocket-server.ts:1252](../packages/server/src/server/websocket-server.ts#L1252)) still fires.
- The client's chunker wraps the relay E2EE `transportFactory` where `daemon-client.ts` reassigns it ([../packages/client/src/daemon-client.ts:1371](../packages/client/src/daemon-client.ts#L1371)), and is imported in `daemon-client.ts` only. It pairs with the carrier transport created inside the same synchronous `transportFactory(...)` call ([../packages/client/src/daemon-client.ts:1378](../packages/client/src/daemon-client.ts#L1378)), which the relay package's client factory records, and reads the negotiation result and drives cutover through that carrier. `daemon-client-relay-e2ee-transport.ts` does not change. With no carrier recorded in that call, it passes through, including for the default `DaemonClient` builds when the CLI passes only a `webSocketFactory`. Direct connections are not wrapped.
- `packages/relay` gains no workspace dependency. The chunker and carrier declare their shapes locally, as the relay's `Transport` does ([../packages/relay/src/encrypted-channel.ts:21](../packages/relay/src/encrypted-channel.ts#L21)), structurally matching the client's `DaemonTransportFactory`, the server's `RelayWebSocketLike` for the carrier-wrapped socket `attachEncryptedSocket` takes ([../packages/server/src/server/relay-transport.ts:417](../packages/server/src/server/relay-transport.ts#L417)), and `RelaySocketLike` for the chunker-wrapped socket handed to `attachSocket`.
- The chunker consumes its own signaling and cutover messages, which travel encrypted on the relay leg and never reach upstream's message handling. No plaintext frame of ours reaches the E2EE channel, other than the capability on `e2ee_hello` / `e2ee_ready`.
- Every encrypted chunk, after E2EE overhead, fits the smaller of 64 KiB and the DataChannel's max message size. The DataChannel is ordered and reliable. The chunker never retries.
- On the DataChannel, the carrier sends only while the channel's buffered amount is under a ceiling below 16 MiB, the WebRTC library's send-queue limit in Chromium ([sctp_data_channel.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/external/webrtc/+/refs/heads/main/pc/sctp_data_channel.cc)), and queues the rest until it drains.
- A daemon that cannot load node-datachannel, a browser without `RTCPeerConnection`, or Android below 13 does not offer the capability and runs on relay. A daemon log line says whether node-datachannel loaded.
- The desktop app's bundled daemon loads node-datachannel under `ELECTRON_RUN_AS_NODE` (`packages/desktop/src/daemon/node-entrypoint-launcher.ts`).
- No new config fields. Each end derives `stun:<relay host>:3478` from its own relay endpoint; on the daemon, `relayEndpoint` ([../packages/server/src/server/relay-transport.ts:351](../packages/server/src/server/relay-transport.ts#L351)). A peer config holds exactly that one STUN URL, and no TURN.
- After cutover both carriers close the relay leg, and that close never reaches the E2EE channel; the DataChannel alone decides when the connection ends. Each transport keeps its own collection of DataChannel connections beside `dataSockets` ([../packages/server/src/server/relay-transport.ts:124](../packages/server/src/server/relay-transport.ts#L124)), and `stop()` ([../packages/server/src/server/relay-transport.ts:152](../packages/server/src/server/relay-transport.ts#L152)) closes them. An entry leaves the collection when its own DataChannel closes, as a `dataSockets` entry does ([../packages/server/src/server/relay-transport.ts:395](../packages/server/src/server/relay-transport.ts#L395)), with no upstream lines beyond those in the table. `ensureClientDataSocket` ([../packages/server/src/server/relay-transport.ts:345](../packages/server/src/server/relay-transport.ts#L345)) skips any id in that collection, so a later `sync` or `connected` never reopens a relay leg for a connection on the DataChannel.
- The Settings label is read in `ConnectionRow`, where the title is built ([../packages/app/src/screens/settings/host-page.tsx:517](../packages/app/src/screens/settings/host-page.tsx#L517)), and the badge in `HostStatusBadges` ([../packages/app/src/screens/settings/host-page.tsx:198](../packages/app/src/screens/settings/host-page.tsx#L198)). Both read the store, keyed by server id and connection id ([../packages/app/src/runtime/host-runtime.ts:1902](../packages/app/src/runtime/host-runtime.ts#L1902)), and redraw when it changes, as the app's other shared stores do ([../packages/app/src/stores/download-store.ts:59](../packages/app/src/stores/download-store.ts#L59)). The call site passes `host.serverId` to the row ([../packages/app/src/screens/settings/host-page.tsx:448](../packages/app/src/screens/settings/host-page.tsx#L448)). `WebRTC` is a literal, as `TCP` is in `formatHostConnectionLabel`.
- A daemon log line names the connection when it cuts over.
- No upstream test file changes, except 1 import and 1 call in `packages/desktop/e2e/packaged-app-smoke.js`, and 1 line in `packages/app/e2e/support/helpers/relay-deployment.ts` that removes `RTCPeerConnection` from the relay restart test's browser.

## Risks

- No measured success rate for STUN-only on carrier NAT.
- A relay endpoint set as an IP address gives STUN only over that address family, since a STUN reply carries the request's own source address ([RFC 8489 §6.3.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8489#section-6.3.1)); a hostname is looked up for both A and AAAA records ([RFC 8489 §8](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8489#section-8)). Self-hosters need a hostname with both A and AAAA records for both families.
- The Expo module over libdatachannel is new ground here.
- `tel.schich:libdatachannel-java-android` loads only on Android 13 and newer: its Java layer ([jar](https://repo1.maven.org/maven2/tel/schich/libdatachannel-java/0.24.1.1/)) uses `java.lang.ref.Cleaner`, added in API 33 ([Cleaner](https://developer.android.com/reference/java/lang/ref/Cleaner)), core library desugaring does not supply it ([support table](https://developer.android.com/studio/write/java8-support-table)), and every release with an Android artifact uses it ([versions](https://repo1.maven.org/maven2/tel/schich/libdatachannel-java-android/maven-metadata.xml)). The app's minimum is Android 10 (API 29). Android 10-12, about 17% of Android web traffic in September 2026 ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/android-version-market-share/mobile-tablet/worldwide)), stays on relay.
- F-Droid's Inclusion Policy allows prebuilt FLOSS binaries from Maven Central, with conditions ([Inclusion Policy](https://f-droid.org/docs/Inclusion_Policy/)), and Mattermost's F-Droid recipe already swaps in a prebuilt WebRTC library from there, `im.conversations.webrtc:webrtc-android` ([recipe](https://gitlab.com/fdroid/fdroiddata/-/raw/master/metadata/com.mattermost.rnbeta.yml)). The library's POM says MIT ([POM](https://repo1.maven.org/maven2/tel/schich/libdatachannel-java-android/0.24.1.1/libdatachannel-java-android-0.24.1.1.pom)) while its source says MPL 2.0 ([LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pschichtel/libdatachannel-java/main/LICENSE)); both are FLOSS.
- The Maven AAR's `.so` files are 25-31 MB per ABI uncompressed, about 8 MB compressed ([AAR](https://repo1.maven.org/maven2/tel/schich/libdatachannel-java-android/0.24.1.1/libdatachannel-java-android-0.24.1.1.aar)); the CocoaPod is versioned 0.24.4 but vendors HaishinKit's prebuilt v0.24.0 xcframework ([podspec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CocoaPods/Specs/master/Specs/5/9/6/libdatachannel/0.24.4/libdatachannel.podspec.json)), and is untried here.
- node-datachannel's native binary must load on every platform the CLI daemon runs on; a failed load falls back to relay.
- Send-queue limits on Safari, Firefox, and the phone module are unverified; the carrier's ceiling is set from Chromium's.
- The Windows arm64 desktop artifact is built on an x64 host and never smoked ([../packages/desktop/scripts/after-pack.js:124](../packages/desktop/scripts/after-pack.js#L124)); if node-datachannel fails to load there, that daemon stays on relay.
